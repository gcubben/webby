New York City

Mantra: People are always looking for something worth believing in. It’s why I get out of bed in the morning. It’s also why I spend $8 on coffee when there’s a perfectly good pot at home. Belief is behind it all. At Duke, I studied economics and religion, which sounds like a peculiar pairing, but it turns out they’re both about believing in value. Where it comes from, who decides it and why people give everything up to have it.

Most of our days revolve around what will make it better. Finding that answer that makes everything else make sense. That’s how I see advertising, too. People are searching, and half the time they don’t even know what for. Advertising gives them something not only worth choosing, but something worth believing in. Because when the idea is right and the words reflect real value, it can become something much bigger than the product. I help make those kind of connections happen. And hopefully leave a few things better than I found them.

Because belief is what moves us →

Even 80 years after Maslow broke down human motivation, most marketers still don’t get it. In 2016, Bain & Company used his framework to show which elements of value shift consumer behavior from “I need this product” to “I want this brand.” The problem is too many brands are still stuck at the bottom of the pyramid, obsessing over functionality and calling it a day. Ask yourself: if your product disappeared tomorrow, would your customers even notice, or just move on? Apple sells the blue text bubbles that make users feel exclusive. Jeep sells a 4-wheel driving game of duck duck goose. Meeting functional needs is just the buy in these days. You have to aim higher if you want to win.

If our days revolve around what will make it better →

Welcome to the rise of new wellness. With viral hacks and trendy pills all promising the big fix, the space has felt a little crowded to say the least. Our client, Kuya, is a holistic wellness center that specializes in a stacked, multi-modality approach that offers real depth comparatively, but left them struggling to claim a clear lane in the game. Deeper research revealed audiences were already paying premium prices for multiple separate wellness memberships, yet Kuya provided that entire ecosystem under one roof. The strategic hurdle was creating a position that balanced science-backed clinical credibility with their distinct, avant-garde bohemian identity. They couldn’t speak to the category because it didn’t exist yet. So we created our own. Kuya: Healthcare for your nervous system. By reframing their offerings from a random suite of services into a tailored, expert-guided path for nervous system regulation, we bridged the gap between clinical efficacy and holistic lifestyle wellness. Kuya. Remeet You.

May 12

Advertising should give them something worth believing in →

Most people view utilities as entities, not brands. They’re invisible services that only earn attention when something goes wrong. But, as North Carolina’s largest municipal electric system, our client Public Works Commission (PWC), wanted to be visible for all the good they do across the state. Its hometown, Fayetteville, is an all-American city with a small-town personality. After some time with the city, we found they were a backbone of the community with an outdated communication model. They relied on one-off, subject-driven broadcasts detailing PWC’s daily operations rather than their impact. Real brand affinity would only come from shifting the narrative from a transactional push of “what we do” to a value-driven story of “why it matters.” They needed a connected framework that could show off how much power they really bring back to the community.

And hopefully leaves a few things better than it found them →

How does a regional theater in rural North Carolina scale its revenue to support a $25 million expansion? This is the question our client Cape Fear Regional Theater, which consistently rivaled bigger hubs like Raleigh and Durham, came to us with. Not only did it have a location-bound name that kept them feeling small, but it also had a linear way to bring in profits that kept them too reliant on donations. In a modern entertainment market driven by multi-experience spaces like food halls and pop-up venues, the theater needed to redefine what it offered and drive new revenue streams. We advised transforming the enlarged facility into a vibrant, multi-functional hub complete with an integrated bar, restaurant, and versatile event spaces capable of hosting year-round programming. And as we conducted interviews, we realized the brand’s true spirit lay in its deep generational roots. Local families grew up on these stages across every season of their lives. To break free from geographic limits, we shed the regional title and rebranded the institution as Stages. The name bridges the literal physical stage with the personal stages of life that every child, parent, and grandparent experiences within its walls. By framing the brand around lifelong artistic connection, Stages unlocked a scalable, modern identity capable of filling seats, expanding revenue, and anchoring the region’s cultural future.
