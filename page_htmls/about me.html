The rise of new wellness. Wellness used to mean just one or two simple things: going to the gym and eating healthy. But as the wellness industry has combusted, the market has become overrun with viral hacks and trendy pills all promising the big fix. Kuya’s stacked, multi-modality approach offered real depth comparatively, but it also left them struggling to claim a single, clear lane. Deeper research revealed audiences were already paying premium prices for multiple separate wellness memberships, yet Kuya provided that entire ecosystem under one roof. The strategic hurdle was creating a position that balanced science-backed clinical credibility with their distinct, avant-garde bohemian identity, all while empowering the customer to take agency over their own health. They couldn’t speak to the category because it didn’t exist yet. So we created our own. Kuya: Healthcare for your nervous system. By reframing their offerings from a random suite of services into a tailored, expert-guided path for nervous system regulation, we bridged the gap between clinical efficacy and holistic lifestyle wellness. Kuya. Remeet You.

Homemaking the hometown utility. Most people view utilities as entities, not brands. They’re invisible services that only earn attention when something goes wrong. A sudden outage or a big bill hike. As North Carolina’s largest municipal electric system, Public Works Commission (PWC), wanted to be visible for all the good they do across the state. Its hometown, Fayetteville, is an all-American city with a small-town personality. After some time with the city, we found they were a backbone of the community with an outdated communication model. They relied on one-off, subject-driven broadcasts detailing PWC's daily operations rather than their impact. Real brand affinity would only come from shifting the narrative from a transactional push of "what we do" to a value-driven story of "why it matters." They needed a connected framework that proved they not only power the community, they help build it.

A name for every stage of growth. How does a regional theater in rural North Carolina scale its revenue to support a $25 million expansion? This is the question Cape Fear Regional Theater, which consistently rivaled bigger hubs like Raleigh and Durham, came to us with. Not only did it have a location-bound name that kept them feeling small, but it also had a linear way to bring in profits that kept them too reliant on donations. In a modern entertainment market driven by multi-experience spaces like food halls and pop-up venues, the theater needed to redefine what it offered. They had already built trust in their artistic excellence; now they needed to leverage that existing trust into becoming more than just a theater and, more so, a competitive entertainment destination. To support this shift and drive new revenue streams, we advised transforming the enlarged facility into a vibrant, multi-functional hub complete with an integrated bar, restaurant, and versatile event spaces capable of hosting year-round programming. As we conducted interviews, we realized the brand’s true spirit lay in its deep generational roots. Local families grew up on these stages across every season of their lives. To break free from geographic limits, we shed the regional title and rebranded the institution as Stages. The name bridges the literal physical stage with the personal stages of life that every child, parent, and grandparent experiences within its walls. By framing the brand around lifelong artistic connection, Stages unlocked a scalable, modern identity capable of filling seats, expanding revenue, and anchoring the region’s cultural future.
